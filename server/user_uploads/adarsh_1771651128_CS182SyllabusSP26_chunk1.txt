### Course Description
This course provides a broad introduction to fundamental discrete mathematical tools, facts, and reasoning relevant to computer science, covering:
- Logic
- Proof methods
- Functions, sums, and sets
- Relations
- Number theory
- Algorithms and growth functions
- Induction, recursions, and recursive algorithms
- Counting
- Basic probability theory
- Trees and graphs
- Finite state automata, regular expressions, and context-free languages
- Turing machines, computable and uncomputable functions

### Learning Resources, Technology, and Texts
- **Required Texts:**
  - K. Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications*, McGraw-Hill; 7th or 8th edition.
  - The textbook's Student Site provides additional resources, examples, self-assessments, and proof guides.
- **Technology Platforms:** The course will utilize Brightspace, Gradescope, and Ed Discussion.

### Learning Outcomes
Upon completing the course, students will be able to:
- Apply principles of discrete mathematics to analyze and solve complex problems.
- Analyze and evaluate the complexity of algorithms using asymptotic notation (Big O, Big Θ, and Big Ω).
- Apply graph theory concepts and algorithms to solve real-world problems including network design and shortest path analysis.
