**CS 381: Fundamental Algorithms Course Overview**

The CS 381 course, offered by the Computer Science department, focuses on the study of fundamental algorithms. This undergraduate course covers a range of topics, including algorithm design and analysis, and is similar to the Spring 2025 course available at [Fundamental Algorithms](https://fundamentalalgorithms.com/fas24). The course utilizes various resources, such as lecture notes, textbooks, and online materials, to provide students with a comprehensive understanding of algorithms.

###

**Key Resources and Textbooks**

- Lecture notes, usually one chapter per lecture, are provided to students.
- Recommended textbooks include:
  - Jeff Erickson's "Algorithms" (2019)
  - Jon M. Kleinberg and Éva Tardos' "Algorithm Design" (2006)
  - Sanjoy Dasgupta, Christos H. Papadimitriou, and Umesh V. Vazirani's "Algorithms" (2008)
  - Thomas H. Cormen, Charles E. Leiserson, Ronald L. Rivest, and Clifford Stein's "Introduction to Algorithms" (2009)
- Online resources, such as video lectures from MIT's 6.006 and 6.046 courses, are also available to supplement course material.

###

**Course Structure and Policies**

- The course consists of lectures, homework assignments, and exams.
- Homework assignments are due weekly, with a late policy in place.
- Exams, including midterms and a final, are a combination of multiple-choice and word problems.
- Grades are curved, with a weighting of:
  - 25% Homework
  - 20% Midterm 1
  - 25% Midterm 2
  - 30% Final
- Collaboration is allowed, and students are encouraged to interact with each other and with instructors during office hours and through EdStem.

###

**Grading and Assessment**

- Homework submissions are collected online through Gradescope.
- Gradescope linke: https://www.gradescope.com/courses/1216667
- Word problems are graded, with the bottom one-fifth of scores dropped.
- Late submissions are accepted, with a penalty of 35% of the total points.
- Exemplary submissions may be selected as solutions, with 10% extra credit awarded.
- Regrade requests must be initiated within one week of grades being returned.

This course is designed to provide students with a solid foundation in fundamental algorithms, with an emphasis on problem-solving, communication, and collaboration. By utilizing a range of resources and assessment methods, the course aims to equip students with the skills and knowledge necessary to succeed in the field of computer science.

###

**Additional Information on LaTeX and Academic Integrity**

Even if you do not know LaTeX now, you probably have to learn it sooner or later (and certainly if you pursue graduate studies). The instructor uses emacs to write LaTeX, but any editor will do. There is also a website called [Overleaf](https://www.overleaf.com) for typesetting LaTeX. Alternatively, the open-source software Marktext allows one to write LaTeX within a markdown document, which is particularly easy to use. LyX is another popular LaTeX editor that is WYSIWYG. There are several apps for scanning documents (e.g., when inserting pictures) that are much better than taking a photo. The instructor uses Scanbot, and other popular apps include Microsoft Office Lens, CamScanner, and Evernote Scannable.

###

**Academic Integrity**

Behavior consistent with cheating, copying, and academic dishonesty is not tolerated. Depending on the severity, this may result in a zero score on the assignment or exam, and could result in a failing grade for the class or even expulsion. Purdue prohibits “dishonesty in connection with any University activity. Cheating, plagiarism, or knowingly furnishing false information to the University are examples of dishonesty” (Part 5, Section III-B-2-a, University Regulations). Furthermore, the University Senate has stipulated that “the commitment of acts of cheating, lying, and deceit in any of their diverse forms (such as the use of substitutes for taking examinations, the use of illegal cribs, plagiarism, and copying during examinations) is dishonest and must not be tolerated. Moreover, knowingly to aid and abet, directly or indirectly, other parties in committing dishonest acts is in itself dishonest” (University Senate Document 7218, December 15, 1972).

You are expected to read both Purdue’s guide to academic integrity at [Purdue Integrity Statement](http://www.purdue.edu/purdue/about/integrity_statement.html) and Prof. Gene Spafford’s guide at [Spaf’s Integrity Guide](http://spaf.cerias.purdue.edu/integrity.html) as well. You are responsible for understanding their contents and how it applies to this class.
