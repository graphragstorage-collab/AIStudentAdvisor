### Course Description  
This course introduces foundational discrete–mathematics tools for computer science, covering topics such as:  
- Logic  
- Proof methods  
- Functions  
- Sets  
- Relations  
- Number theory  
- Algorithm analysis  
- Induction  
- Counting  
- Probability  
- Trees/graphs  
- Finite-state automata  
- Regular expressions  
- Context-free languages  
- Turing machines  
- Computability  

### Required Text  
- K. Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications* (7th/8th ed.) - The student site includes examples, self-assessment resources, and a proof-writing guide.  

### Learning Outcomes  
Students will be able to:  
- Apply discrete mathematics principles.  
- Analyze algorithm complexity (Big O, Θ, Ω).  
- Use graph theory and algorithms to solve real-world problems.

### Conduct and Courtesy  
Students must maintain a professional and respectful classroom environment, including:  
- No talking during lectures.  
- Silencing personal electronics.  
- Arriving on time and remaining for the duration of the class.  
- Treating others with respect.  

### Netiquette  
When using Ed Discussion for communication, abide by the following standards:  
- Be polite and courteous.  
- Avoid using all caps (equivalent to screaming).  
- No offensive language or disruptive behavior.  
- Use clear subject lines and avoid abbreviations or acronyms not understood by all.  
- Keep discussions professional and collegial, avoiding inappropriate material or solutions to homework and exam questions.  

### AI Policy  
Students may use AI as a learning tool for understanding concepts and generating exercises. However, AI usage for completing homework assignments or during quizzes is not permitted.  

### Academic Integrity  
The course expects adherence to academic honesty policies, which state:  
- Homework solutions must be original work, with proper credit given for any online material used.  
- Sharing or copying solutions is considered cheating.  
- All instances of academic dishonesty will be reported to the Dean of Students Office, with penalties including zeros for assignments or possible failure of the course.
