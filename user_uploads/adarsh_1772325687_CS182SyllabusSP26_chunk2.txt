### Course Description
CS 18200 provides introductory coverage of discrete mathematics tools and reasoning for computer science, including:
- Logic
- Proof methods
- Functions
- Sets
- Number theory
- Algorithms
- Induction
- Counting
- Probability
- Graph theory
- Finite-state automata
- Regular expressions
- Context-free languages
- Turing machines
- Computability

### Learning Outcomes
Upon completion, students will be able to:
- Apply discrete-math foundations
- Analyze algorithms (Big-O/Θ/Ω)
- Solve complex problems using graph-theoretic algorithms

### Required Text
- K. Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications* (7th/8th edition) with accompanying student site resources.

### Attendance Policy
Students are expected to:
- Attend all classes and in-person exams unless ill or have excused absences (grief/bereavement, military service, jury duty, parenting leave, medically excused absences).
- Notify course coordinators in advance for anticipated conflicts.

### Conduct and Courtesy
Students must maintain a professional and respectful classroom environment:
- No talking during lectures.
- Silencing personal electronics.
- Arriving on time and staying for the entire class.
- Treating others with respect.
- Non-disruptive personal electronics are allowed.

### Netiquette Guidelines
- Be polite and courteous.
- Reflect on what others are saying.
- Avoid all caps, flames, and offensive language.
- Use clear subject lines and avoid abbreviations unless known to the entire class.
- Keep discussions collegial and professional.
- Do not post inappropriate material or solutions to homework/exam problems.

### AI Policy
Students may use AI as a tool for learning but are not permitted to use AI to complete homework assignments or during quizzes.

### Academic Integrity
Adhere to Purdue’s academic honesty policies as described at [Purdue Academic Integrity Policy](http://spaf.cerias.purdue.edu/cpolicy.html). 
- Students must submit their own work and correctly reference any material used from online sources.
- Sharing or copying solutions is considered cheating.  

### Penalties
- First instance of academic dishonesty: zero for that assignment + letter grade deduction.
- Second instance: course grade of F.
- Instances of dishonesty on exams result in a course grade of F and reporting to the Dean of Students Office.

### Copyright Expectations
All course materials are the instructor’s property. Students may use course materials for their own use but cannot distribute without written consent.
